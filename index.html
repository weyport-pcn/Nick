The space before demand

Services see people once they become demand. Before that, people search.

A council, a GP practice or a charity understands demand well once it arrives as a referral, an appointment or a call. Before that comes a quieter moment. A bill that can't be paid. A parent wondering whether their child needs help. Feeling low for weeks. Most people pick up their phone and search.

That moment is the closest any service gets to the person. Very few places are listening to it.

Small setbacks rarely stay small. Left alone, they cluster, and a few of them together can tip someone into crisis and a costly emergency admission. Catching them early is kinder for the person and cheaper for everyone.

Civic architecture

Roads let a place move. Libraries let it learn. Signals lets it listen.

Neighbourhood Signals is infrastructure. It reads how people move through the websites built to help them, in aggregate, and shows what a neighbourhood is looking for and struggling to find. Nobody signs up, and nobody is tracked.

No cookiesNothing stored on anyone's phone. No consent banner needed.
No personal dataAggregated behaviour only. Patterns, never people.
No loginsNo accounts and no forms. People just look for help.

Signposting from the reception desk, so front-line staff can point people to the right place.

What Dorset would get

A short, plain-English read for service leads and residents alike. What people searched for, what they couldn't find, and what changed since last time. Published openly, because a neighbourhood should be able to see what it is telling itself.

How it would work

Listen. Learn. Act.

Listen
The council

Add Plausible to the parts of the council website where people go looking for help, starting with four:

  • SEND Local Offer
  • Adult social care
  • Cost of living help
  • Homelessness and housing advice

One line of code. Cookieless and EU-hosted. The council owns the account and the data, and shares read access.

Learn
Neighbourhood CoLab

CoLab is commissioned to interpret the data and publish an open Neighbourhood Brief, starting with Weymouth and Portland.

The model is open. The interpretation is the work. Dorset-wide when the council is ready.

Act
The council and its partners

Services decide what to change, using what residents are already telling them.

CoLab interprets. It never decides. That keeps the Brief trusted.

Next step
